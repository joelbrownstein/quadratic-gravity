# Black holes, Euclidean methods, and quadratic gravity

This reading order supports the manuscript's black-hole discussion. It separates thermal-state construction, classical modified-gravity solutions, and proposals for replacing a singular interior. None of these roles should be substituted for another.

## 1. Establish what the Euclidean construction computes

1. **Hartle and Hawking (1976), “Path-integral derivation of black-hole radiance.”** [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.13.2188). Start with analytic continuation of particle amplitudes and the emission/absorption relation. This is a derivation on a specified background, not a calculation of quantum backreaction resolving its center.

2. **Gibbons and Hawking (1977), “Action integrals and partition functions in quantum gravity.”** [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.15.2752). Follow the relation between complexified geometry, boundary contributions, thermodynamic ensembles, and entropy. Reproduce the Schwarzschild period before introducing an alternative completion.

3. **Hartle and Hawking (1983), “Wave function of the Universe.”** [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.28.2960). Read for the no-boundary state prescription and its cosmological setting. Distinguish this proposal from the black-hole equilibrium state and from a proposed Euclidean interior.

## 2. Check the definition and stability of the integral

4. **Gibbons, Hawking, and Perry (1978), “Path integrals and the indefiniteness of the gravitational action.”** [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(78)90161-X). Identifies the conformal-factor convergence problem. A useful contrast with the nonnegative restricted PS action; the latter does not automatically define the full gravitational contour.

5. **Gross, Perry, and Yaffe (1982), “Instability of flat space at finite temperature.”** [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.25.330). Study the Euclidean Schwarzschild negative mode. Distinguish a smooth stationary point from a stable ensemble and ask how the selected boundary conditions affect the calculation.

## 3. Determine what quadratic gravity changes

6. **Lü, Perkins, Pope, and Stelle (2015), “Black Holes in Higher-Derivative Gravity.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.01028). Establishes additional static black-hole branches while retaining Schwarzschild. This is the key check against claiming that adding quadratic operators removes singular solutions automatically.

7. **Holdom and Ren (2017), “Not quite a black hole.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.04889). Studies matter-supported horizonless 2-2-holes, including a timelike curvature singularity. Keep horizon avoidance, altered propagation, and bounded curvature as distinct questions.

8. **Wald (1993), “Black Hole Entropy is Noether Charge.”** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9307038). Supplies the action-dependent entropy framework for stationary bifurcate horizons. Use it when comparing Einstein and curvature-squared solutions rather than assuming the Einstein area formula universally.

## 4. Evaluate an alternative global completion

9. **Tzanavaris, Boyle, and Turok (2024; revised May 2025), “Black Mirrors: CPT-Symmetric Alternatives to Black Holes.”** [Paper, version 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09558v2). Examine the proposed CPT boundary conditions, bounded curvature, and the acknowledged zeros of metric eigenvalues at the joining horizon. Its admissibility in QQG requires a separate calculation.

10. **Penrose (1965), “Gravitational Collapse and Space-Time Singularities.”** [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.14.57). Return to the assumptions and geodesic-incompleteness conclusion. For any proposed resolution, identify which assumption changes and how physical predictivity is recovered.

## Questions for a proposed Euclidean-interior paper

- Which amplitude, density matrix, or state does the construction define?
- Is the Euclidean region an auxiliary saddle, a physical signature-changing region, or an alternative global completion?
- Are curvature, inverse metric, tidal observables, and physical propagation all controlled, or only selected quantities?
- What action, contour, measure, higher-derivative boundary data, and junction conditions are used?
- Does the replacement follow from collapse or from imposed final boundary conditions?
- Which conclusions require Weyl/tensor dynamics, and which can be tested in a scalar or fixed-background stage?
- Does the calculation establish singularity resolution, or only a candidate geometry and a program for testing it?



## Additions from the reference-first Bond pass (20 November 2026)

- **Held and Zhang, Instability of spherically-symmetric black holes in Quadratic Gravity (2023)**, [arXiv:2209.01867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.01867), [published paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.107.064060). Read after the branch-existence papers: both known spherical branches have a long-wavelength instability below their intersection radius. Linear instability does not determine the nonlinear endpoint.
- **Liu, Quintin and Afshordi, Conformal Cores of Quantum Black Holes in Quadratic Gravity (2025)**, [arXiv:2411.19311v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19311v2), [published paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.111.044031). A direct candidate for the user's interest in complex and Euclidean interiors. Read sections 6.2–7 as well as the construction: the contour avoids a branch cut/singularity, but violates the stated Kontsevich–Segal criterion for arbitrary real matter; the authors discuss possible weakenings. Treat matching, contour admissibility, stability and predictive observables as open tasks.
