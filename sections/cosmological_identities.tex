\section{Metric \texorpdfstring{$f(R)$}{f(R)} identities and interpretation checks}
\label{app:cosmological-identities}

For the Lorentzian metric theory in eq.~\eqref{eq:quadratic-fr}, define $F\equiv f_R=\partial f/\partial R$ and $T_{\mu\nu}=-(2/\sqrt{-g})\delta S_{\rm m}/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$.  Metric variation and contraction give~\cite{DeFeliceTsujikawa:2010}
\begin{align}
 F R_{\mu\nu}-\frac12 f g_{\mu\nu}
 +(g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu)F
 &=M_{\rm Pl}^{-2}T_{\mu\nu},\label{eq:fr-field-equations}\\
 3\Box F+FR-2f&=M_{\rm Pl}^{-2}T.\label{eq:fr-trace}
\end{align}
Here $\Box=g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu$ is Lorentzian; it is not the Euclidean lattice operator.  For $T=0$ and constant $R$, substitution of $f=R-2\Lambda+\gamma R^2$ yields
\begin{equation}
 FR-2f=(1+2\gamma R)R-2(R-2\Lambda+\gamma R^2)
 =-R+4\Lambda.
\end{equation}
This derives eq.~\eqref{eq:constant-curvature-check} without assuming that all solutions have constant curvature.

For conserved pressureless matter and radiation in a spatially flat background, an observer adopting general relativity and a fixed $M_{\rm Pl}$ can define
\begin{align}
 \rho_{\rm DE}^{\rm eff}&=3M_{\rm Pl}^2H^2-\rho_m-\rho_r,\\
 p_{\rm DE}^{\rm eff}&=-M_{\rm Pl}^2(2\dot H+3H^2)-\rho_r/3,
 \qquad w_{\rm DE}^{\rm eff}=p_{\rm DE}^{\rm eff}/\rho_{\rm DE}^{\rm eff}.
\end{align}
These are bookkeeping definitions, valid for that assumed decomposition, with $w_{\rm DE}^{\rm eff}$ undefined at zero effective density.  They do not demonstrate a new material component.  Separating matter and geometry requires additional physical assumptions and observables.

For example, write linear scalar perturbations as
\begin{equation}
 \dd s^2=-(1+2\Psi)\dd t^2
 +a_{\rm cos}^2(t)(1-2\Phi)\dd\boldsymbol{x}^2.
\end{equation}
Nonrelativistic motions respond to $\Psi$, while weak lensing probes the combination $\Phi+\Psi$.  Their joint scale and redshift dependence can constrain a specified modified-gravity theory.  The single conformal field in eq.~\eqref{eq:conformal-metric} must not be identified with an independently adjustable pair $(\Phi,\Psi)$.  A perturbation-level prediction requires the constraints and field content of the full cosmological model.
